## VI. A Note on Sourcing and Disagreement

Where this thesis makes claims about facts (e.g., the precise size of a market, the date of a specific event), the underlying source is generally a regulator's published data, a self-reported corporate filing, or a peer-reviewed academic paper. Where this thesis takes interpretive positions (e.g., whether the GSEs caused the crisis, whether private credit constitutes a systemic risk, whether the post-crisis reforms are net stabilizing), the thesis attempts to present the competing positions with their leading proponents rather than impose a single view. The bibliography above includes representatives of the major contesting interpretations.

Several specific areas where competent analysts disagree:

- **Causal weight of monetary policy vs. global capital flows** in pre-crisis credit creation (Bernanke vs. Taylor; broadly speaking, "savings glut" vs. "Fed too loose").
- **Causal role of the GSEs** (Wallison/Pinto vs. FCIC majority).
- **Effectiveness of post-crisis reforms** (officials and industry trade groups vs. critical academics like Anat Admati).
- **Systemic importance of private credit** (industry vs. central bank and FSB research).
- **Adequacy of resolution authority** for SIFIs (untested empirically).
- **Implicit federal guarantee of non-banks** (operationally extensive; legally contested).

The thesis attempts honest treatment of these disagreements. Readers seeking the canonical authority on contested matters should consult the relevant source documents directly.
