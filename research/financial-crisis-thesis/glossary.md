# Glossary

A reference glossary of the technical terms used in this thesis. Definitions are working operational definitions rather than legal definitions; for legal precision, consult the relevant statute, regulation, or ISDA documentation.

---

## A

**ABCP (Asset-Backed Commercial Paper).** Short-term debt issued by a special-purpose entity, typically secured by receivables (credit card balances, auto loans, mortgages, or asset-backed securities). Maturities typically 1–270 days. Pre-crisis market size peaked at $1.18 trillion (July 2007); fell to under $1 trillion by November 2007 in the funding withdrawal.

**ABS (Asset-Backed Security).** A bond whose payments depend on the cash flows of an underlying pool of receivables. Includes RMBS (residential mortgage), CMBS (commercial mortgage), auto loan ABS, credit card ABS, student loan ABS, and others.

**ABS CDO.** A collateralized debt obligation whose collateral is asset-backed securities, typically the mezzanine tranches of subprime RMBS. The structure responsible for the bulk of 2007–2008 crisis losses.

**ABX.HE.** A series of indices launched in January 2006 by Markit, referencing baskets of 20 subprime MBS deals each. Provided a liquid, tradable short vehicle for subprime credit; widely used by the Big Short investors.

**Adjustable-Rate Mortgage (ARM).** A mortgage whose interest rate adjusts periodically based on a reference index plus a margin. Pre-crisis subprime ARMs typically had a fixed initial period (2 or 3 years) and reset to a substantially higher rate thereafter ("2/28" or "3/27" structures).

**Adverse Selection.** The economic phenomenon in which the parties with the worst information about a transaction's quality migrate disproportionately into the transaction. In the mortgage context, borrowers unable to qualify under prime guidelines migrated into the looser subprime channel, raising the average risk of the subprime pool beyond what observable characteristics would suggest.

**Aggregator.** An institution that purchases loans from multiple originators, aggregates them into pools, and securitizes them. In residential mortgages, typically large investment banks (Bear Stearns, Lehman, Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Merrill Lynch) or large universal banks (Citigroup, Wells Fargo).

**AIG-FP (AIG Financial Products).** A London-based subsidiary of American International Group. Wrote credit default swap protection on approximately $440 billion of notional reference credit, including approximately $80 billion on subprime-related references. Required emergency Federal Reserve support of $85+ billion in September 2008.

**Alt-A.** A category of mortgage borrower between prime and subprime — typically prime FICO scores but with non-traditional documentation, investor-property purchases, or other features that prevented agency eligibility. Approximately $400 billion of Alt-A originations in 2006.

**Amortization.** The scheduled reduction of a loan's principal balance through periodic payments. A "fully amortizing" loan pays off its principal over its term. A "negatively amortizing" loan increases its principal balance as unpaid interest is added.

**Arrangers.** The investment banks that structure and place a securitization or syndicated loan.

---

## B

**Basel I, II, III.** Successive international agreements on banking regulation, named for the city in Switzerland where the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision meets. Basel I (1988): risk-weighted capital requirements with broad categories. Basel II (2004, partially implemented before crisis): more granular risk weights and internal-models approaches. Basel III (2010, phased through 2019): higher capital ratios, leverage ratio, LCR, NSFR. Basel III.endgame: further revisions through 2024 with proposed implementation in 2025.

**BDC (Business Development Company).** A type of closed-end investment company that primarily invests in middle-market private companies, often through debt instruments. Publicly traded BDCs operate at 1.5–2x leverage and distribute most income.

**Big Short.** Colloquial name for the trade of buying credit default swap protection on subprime MBS and CDOs in 2005–2007. Most associated with Michael Burry (Scion), Steve Eisman (FrontPoint), John Paulson (Paulson & Co.), Greg Lippmann (Deutsche Bank), and Cornwall Capital.

**BNPL (Buy Now Pay Later).** A category of short-term consumer credit offered at retail point-of-sale by Affirm, Klarna, Afterpay, and others. Often without traditional credit underwriting; emerged as a meaningful category post-2020.

**Bond Insurance.** A guarantee issued by a "monoline" insurer that the principal and interest payments on a specific bond will be made even if the issuer defaults. Monoline insurers (MBIA, Ambac, FGIC, FSA) wrapped municipal bonds and structured-finance securities. Multiple monolines were downgraded or insolvent in 2008.

**BSL (Broadly Syndicated Loan).** A leveraged corporate loan distributed by a syndicate to institutional investors. Distinguished from "middle-market" or "direct" lending, where loans are originated and held by a smaller set of institutional lenders.

**BTFP (Bank Term Funding Program).** A Federal Reserve facility launched March 13, 2023 (after SVB), allowing depository institutions to borrow against the par (not market) value of high-quality securities. Closed to new lending March 11, 2024.

---

## C

**Carry / Carry Trade.** A strategy of borrowing at a low interest rate and investing in a higher-yielding asset, capturing the spread. The dominant pre-crisis strategy at investment banks; structurally short volatility and short tail risk.

**Case-Shiller Index.** A widely-followed index of U.S. home prices, developed by Karl Case and Robert Shiller. Multiple variants (national, 10-city, 20-city). Index peaked mid-2006, fell ~27% peak-to-trough through 2012.

**Cash Flow Waterfall.** The contractually specified sequence by which cash collections from a securitized pool are distributed to investors in different tranches. Senior tranches receive interest first; principal is paid sequentially top-down; losses are absorbed bottom-up from the equity tranche.

**CCP (Central Counterparty).** A clearing house that interposes itself between the two parties to a derivatives or securities trade, becoming the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer. Major CCPs: LCH (London), CME, ICE, Eurex, JSCC.

**CDO (Collateralized Debt Obligation).** A securitization whose collateral is debt instruments (loans, bonds, or asset-backed securities). Tranched and rated like an RMBS but on different underlying collateral. Subcategories: CLO (loans), ABS CDO (asset-backed securities), CDO-squared (other CDOs).

**CDO-squared.** A CDO whose collateral consists of tranches of other CDOs. The most layered and opaque structure of the pre-crisis era. Multiple CDO-squareds experienced 100% principal loss in 2008.

**CDS (Credit Default Swap).** A bilateral derivative contract in which one party (the protection buyer) pays a periodic premium to the other (the protection seller) in exchange for a payment if a specified credit event occurs on a defined reference credit. Functionally similar to insurance on a bond.

**CET1 (Common Equity Tier 1).** The highest-quality regulatory capital under Basel III, consisting primarily of common stock and retained earnings. The principal capital ratio for assessing major bank capital adequacy.

**CFPB (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau).** A federal agency created by Dodd-Frank (Title X) to regulate consumer financial products. Responsible for implementing Qualified Mortgage rule, mortgage servicing rules, debt collection regulation, and others.

**Clayton Holdings.** A third-party due diligence firm that reviewed loan files for investment banks purchasing mortgage pools pre-crisis. Clayton's loan-level review data, released in FCIC investigation, showed that 28% of sampled loans in 2006–2007 had material exceptions to stated underwriting guidelines; aggregators waived ~39% of those exceptions.

**CLO (Collateralized Loan Obligation).** A securitization whose collateral is leveraged corporate loans. Major post-crisis structured finance category; ~$1.2 trillion U.S. outstanding.

**CLTV (Combined Loan-to-Value).** The total mortgage debt against a property as a percentage of the property's value, including both first and second liens.

**CMBS (Commercial Mortgage-Backed Security).** A bond whose payments depend on the cash flows of a pool of commercial mortgages.

**Conservatorship.** A legal status in which a federal agency takes control of an entity to preserve its operations. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac entered FHFA conservatorship September 7, 2008; they remain in conservatorship in 2026.

**Conduit.** A special-purpose entity that issues ABCP and uses the proceeds to purchase receivables or other assets. Pre-crisis bank-sponsored conduits held substantial mortgage and asset-backed exposure off-balance-sheet.

**Convexity.** The second derivative of a bond's price with respect to its yield — a measure of how duration changes as yields change. MBS have negative convexity due to the prepayment option held by borrowers.

**Countrywide.** A major mortgage originator and servicer. Originated approximately $400 billion of mortgages annually at peak. Acquired by Bank of America in 2008, producing massive subsequent losses.

**CPFF (Commercial Paper Funding Facility).** A Federal Reserve facility launched October 7, 2008, purchasing commercial paper directly from issuers. Peak holdings approximately $350 billion.

**Credit Default Swap.** See CDS.

**Credit Enhancement.** Mechanisms in a securitization that protect senior tranches from loss. Four principal methods: subordination, overcollateralization, excess spread, and external wraps (bond insurance).

**Credit Event.** A defined occurrence that triggers payments under a CDS contract. Typically: bankruptcy, failure to pay, restructuring, principal writedown.

**CRT (Credit Risk Transfer).** A class of securities issued by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (Connecticut Avenue Securities, STACR) that transfer credit risk on pools of conforming mortgages to private investors.

**CSE (Consolidated Supervised Entities).** An SEC program (2004–2008) allowing the major U.S. investment banks to use internal models for net capital calculation. Discontinued after the crisis; the SEC OIG concluded the program failed to adequately supervise model assumptions.

---

## D

**Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (2010).** The principal U.S. legislative response to the 2008 crisis. Multiple titles addressing systemic risk, derivatives regulation, consumer protection, and resolution authority.

**DSCR (Debt-Service Coverage Ratio).** A property's net operating income divided by its debt service. A primary underwriting metric for commercial real estate loans.

**Duration.** The first derivative of a bond's price with respect to its yield — a measure of how price changes when yields change by a small amount. Often expressed in "years."

---

## E

**EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization).** A measure of operating performance often used as the denominator in leverage and coverage ratios. Subject to extensive "addbacks" in leveraged finance documentation.

**Equity Tranche.** The lowest-rated (or unrated) tranche in a securitization, absorbing losses first. Sometimes retained by the issuer; sometimes sold to hedge funds and other risk-taking investors. Pays the residual cash flow after senior tranches.

**Excess Spread.** In a securitization, the difference between the weighted-average coupon on the collateral and the weighted-average coupon on the issued bonds, after servicing fees. A primary source of credit enhancement.

**EPD (Early Payment Default).** A loan that becomes delinquent within a short period after origination (typically 60+ days within 90 days). Pre-crisis aggregator-originator contracts typically required the originator to repurchase EPD loans.

---

## F

**Fannie Mae.** Federal National Mortgage Association. A GSE that purchases conforming mortgages and issues MBS guaranteed by Fannie. In federal conservatorship since September 2008.

**FCIC (Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission).** A bipartisan commission established by Congress to investigate the causes of the 2008 financial crisis. Final report issued January 2011.

**FDIC (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation).** The U.S. federal agency that insures deposits at participating banks and resolves failing depositories.

**FHA (Federal Housing Administration).** A federal agency that insures mortgage loans made by approved lenders to lower-credit-quality borrowers and lower-down-payment borrowers. FHA-insured loans are securitized through Ginnie Mae.

**FHFA (Federal Housing Finance Agency).** The federal agency created by HERA (2008) that supervises Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and the Federal Home Loan Banks. Exercises conservatorship authority over Fannie and Freddie.

**FICO Score.** A credit score developed by Fair Isaac Corporation, widely used in U.S. consumer credit underwriting. Ranges from 300 to 850; pre-crisis subprime defined as <660.

**FOMC (Federal Open Market Committee).** The committee within the Federal Reserve System that sets monetary policy.

**Freddie Mac.** Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation. A GSE structurally similar to Fannie Mae; in federal conservatorship since September 2008.

---

## G

**Gaussian Copula.** A statistical model used to capture joint default behavior in a portfolio of credits, parameterized by a correlation parameter. Widely used in CDO pricing pre-crisis. Critiqued for assuming thin-tailed Gaussian joint distributions and for static (rather than state-dependent) correlation.

**Ginnie Mae.** Government National Mortgage Association. A federal corporation that guarantees the timely payment of principal and interest on pools of FHA/VA-insured mortgage loans. Full faith and credit of the U.S. government.

**G-SIB (Global Systemically Important Bank).** A bank designated by the Financial Stability Board as systemically important globally. G-SIBs face additional capital surcharges and supervisory requirements. The 2024 list includes 29 banks; JPMorgan Chase in the highest "bucket 4."

**GSE (Government-Sponsored Enterprise).** A federally chartered corporation with implicit federal support. In mortgage finance: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.

---

## H

**Haircut.** In repo and securities lending, the percentage by which the loan amount is less than the market value of the collateral. A 2% haircut on $100 million of Treasury collateral means the lender provides $98 million in cash.

**HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit).** A revolving credit facility secured by a junior lien on the borrower's home, typically with a 10-year draw period and a 10–20 year repayment period.

**HOEPA (Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act, 1994).** Federal law granting the Federal Reserve authority to restrict abusive lending practices. The Fed exercised this authority only modestly until 2008.

**HQLA (High-Quality Liquid Assets).** Under Basel III LCR, assets that can be reliably converted to cash quickly to meet liquidity needs. Tiered into Level 1 (Treasuries, central bank reserves), Level 2A (agency debt, high-quality corporates), and Level 2B (some equities, lower-rated corporates).

**HTM (Held-to-Maturity).** An accounting classification for debt securities under which the security is carried at amortized cost (not market value), with unrealized gains and losses not flowing through income or capital. Subject to disclosure of mark-to-market values. The HTM classification was the proximate accounting cause of SVB's run.

**Hybrid ARM.** An adjustable-rate mortgage with an extended initial fixed-rate period followed by floating-rate periods. Pre-crisis subprime typical: 2/28 (2 fixed, 28 floating), 3/27, 5/25.

---

## I

**Initial Margin (IM).** Collateral posted at the inception of a derivatives transaction to cover potential future losses on the position. Distinguished from variation margin, which addresses realized mark-to-market movement.

**Interest-Only (I/O) Loan.** A loan that requires only interest payments during an initial period, with no principal amortization. Pre-crisis: substantial fraction of Alt-A originations. Currently: rare in residential, common in commercial real estate.

**ISDA (International Swaps and Derivatives Association).** The trade association responsible for the standardized documentation governing OTC derivatives, including the Master Agreement and Credit Support Annex (CSA).

---

## J

**Jumbo.** A mortgage with a principal balance exceeding the GSE conforming limit (varies by year and metropolitan area; $766,550 in most areas for 2024). Jumbo loans are not GSE-eligible and are funded either by bank portfolio retention or private-label securitization.

---

## L

**LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio).** A Basel III requirement that banks hold sufficient HQLA to cover 30 days of stressed net cash outflows. Minimum 100%.

**LCFI (Large Complex Financial Institution).** A pre-crisis term for the major investment banks and universal banks; replaced post-crisis by SIFI and G-SIB designations.

**Lehman Brothers.** A major U.S. investment bank that filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 15, 2008 — the largest bankruptcy in U.S. history at $639 billion in assets.

**Leveraged Loan.** A bank-syndicated corporate loan to a non-investment-grade borrower, typically floating-rate with senior secured ranking.

**LIBOR (London Interbank Offered Rate).** The rate at which major banks reported willingness to lend to each other on unsecured terms. The principal reference rate for floating-rate instruments pre-crisis. Phased out in 2021–2023 in favor of SOFR (USD), SONIA (GBP), and other alternatives.

**LIBOR-OIS Spread.** The spread between 3-month LIBOR and the overnight indexed swap rate. A key indicator of interbank funding stress; widened from ~10 bps to ~365 bps at crisis peak.

**Lippmann, Greg.** Head of CDO trading at Deutsche Bank; the most aggressive seller of CDS protection on subprime to outside hedge funds during 2005–2006 while accumulating Deutsche's own short subprime position.

**Lock-In Effect.** The reluctance of borrowers with low-rate mortgages to sell their homes in a higher-rate environment because doing so would require new mortgage at the current (higher) rate. A defining feature of the post-2022 U.S. housing market.

**LTV (Loan-to-Value).** A loan's principal balance as a percentage of the property's appraised value.

---

## M

**Maiden Lane I, II, III.** Special-purpose vehicles created by the Federal Reserve in 2008 to hold troubled assets — Maiden Lane I (Bear Stearns mortgage assets), Maiden Lane II (AIG securities lending portfolio), Maiden Lane III (AIG super-senior CDO purchase).

**Mark-to-Market.** Valuing an asset at its current market price rather than at historical cost. Required for most trading-book assets at financial institutions under accounting rules.

**Mark-to-Model.** Valuing an asset using an internal pricing model rather than observable market prices. Required for assets without observable market prices. Subject to extensive judgment in model selection and parameter choice.

**MBS (Mortgage-Backed Security).** A bond whose payments depend on the cash flows of a pool of mortgages. Major categories: agency MBS (GSE-guaranteed), non-agency / private-label MBS.

**Mezzanine.** Tranches of a securitization that are subordinate to senior tranches but senior to the equity tranche. Typically rated A, BBB, or BB. Pre-crisis subprime MBS mezzanine tranches were the primary input to ABS CDOs.

**MMF (Money Market Fund).** A mutual fund that invests in short-term, high-quality debt instruments and seeks to maintain a constant $1.00 net asset value. Total industry assets approximately $7–8 trillion globally.

**Monoline.** A specialty insurance company that focuses on a single line of business — in the relevant context, bond insurance.

**Moral Hazard.** The economic phenomenon in which insurance against losses changes the insured party's behavior to take more risk. Recurring concern in financial crisis response and policy design.

**MSR (Mortgage Servicing Right).** The contractual right and obligation to service a mortgage, including a fee for performing the servicing. MSRs are valued on the basis of expected prepayment behavior and discount rates.

---

## N

**NBFI (Non-Bank Financial Intermediation).** Credit intermediation activities performed outside the regulated banking system. The FSB's annual NBFI monitoring report tracks the size and structure of this sector.

**NINJA Loan.** "No Income, No Job, No Assets" — an industry shorthand for loans approved without documentation of any underwriting variable. The most extreme form of relaxed underwriting in the pre-crisis era.

**Non-Agency / Private-Label.** Mortgage securitizations issued by private institutions without GSE or federal guarantee. Distinguished from agency MBS issued or guaranteed by Fannie, Freddie, or Ginnie.

**Non-QM.** Mortgages that do not meet the CFPB's Qualified Mortgage standard. A small post-crisis successor category to subprime, serving borrowers (especially self-employed) with non-traditional income documentation but typically with strong other underwriting characteristics.

**NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio).** A Basel III requirement that banks have sufficient stable funding (deposits, longer-dated debt, equity) to support their assets and off-balance-sheet exposures over a 1-year horizon.

---

## O

**OAS (Option-Adjusted Spread).** The spread of an MBS over the Treasury curve after adjusting for the value of the embedded prepayment option. The principal valuation metric for agency MBS.

**OC (Overcollateralization).** The amount by which a securitization's collateral exceeds the principal balance of the bonds it has issued. A form of credit enhancement.

**OFR (Office of Financial Research).** A Treasury Department research office created by Dodd-Frank to support the FSOC's macroprudential mission.

**OLA (Orderly Liquidation Authority).** The resolution framework for systemically important financial institutions established under Dodd-Frank Title II. Untested at a major institution as of 2026.

**Originate-to-Distribute.** A business model in which a lender originates loans for the purpose of selling them to a third party, rather than holding them on balance sheet through maturity. Pre-crisis dominant for mortgages.

**Originate-to-Hold.** A business model in which a lender originates loans for the purpose of holding them through maturity. Traditional commercial banking; substantially displaced by originate-to-distribute pre-crisis.

**OTC (Over-the-Counter).** Bilateral derivatives or securities traded directly between counterparties, rather than on an exchange. Pre-crisis, the dominant model for CDS and most other credit derivatives.

---

## P

**Pass-Through Security.** A simple structured product in which each investor owns a pro-rata share of all cash flows from a pool of underlying loans. Distinguished from tranched securities that allocate cash flows by priority.

**Paulson, John.** Founder of Paulson & Co. Made approximately $15 billion of personal/firm profit in 2007–2008 on the Big Short trade. Worked with Goldman Sachs to construct synthetic CDOs (Abacus 2007-AC1) in which Paulson selected reference collateral with the intent to short.

**Pay-As-You-Go (PAUG) CDS.** A specific ISDA template for CDS on ABS reference entities, with floating recovery and payments tied to realized cash flow shortfalls on the reference.

**Pay-Option ARM.** An adjustable-rate mortgage offering the borrower a choice of monthly payment options each month, including a "minimum payment" that may be less than the accrued interest, leading to negative amortization. Pre-crisis ~$750 billion outstanding at peak.

**PDCF (Primary Dealer Credit Facility).** A Federal Reserve facility launched March 16, 2008, allowing primary dealers to borrow directly from the Fed's discount window. The first time the Fed had extended its lender-of-last-resort function to non-bank dealers since the Great Depression.

**Ponzi Finance.** In Hyman Minsky's typology, a financing posture in which asset cash flows are insufficient even to service interest, requiring asset price appreciation or external financing to maintain the position.

**Pooling and Servicing Agreement (PSA).** The contract governing a securitization trust, including the rules for the cash flow waterfall, the servicer's obligations, and the rights of bondholders.

**Pre-Funding.** A securitization feature in which investors purchase certificates against a pool whose final composition will be determined after the initial closing (typically within 60–90 days). Used to accelerate origination-to-securitization timelines.

**Prepayment Penalty.** A fee charged to a borrower who prepays a loan before a specified date. Pre-crisis subprime typical: 80% of 6 months' interest for 24–36 months. Restricted under Dodd-Frank for residential mortgages.

**Prime.** The highest borrower credit quality tier — high FICO scores, low LTV, fully documented income.

---

## Q

**QE (Quantitative Easing).** Federal Reserve purchases of longer-dated assets (Treasuries, agency MBS) to lower long-term interest rates and support credit conditions. Four programs to date: QE1 (2008–2010), QE2 (2010–2011), QE3 (2012–2014), QE4 (2020–2022).

**QM (Qualified Mortgage).** A category of mortgage loans defined by the CFPB that automatically satisfies the lender's ability-to-repay obligation under Dodd-Frank. The QM framework eliminates most pre-crisis high-risk product features.

**QRM (Qualified Residential Mortgage).** A related but distinct definition, principally used to determine which mortgages are exempt from the 5% retention requirement under Dodd-Frank Section 941. Aligned with QM in implementing regulations.

---

## R

**Rating Agency.** A firm that assesses the creditworthiness of bonds and other obligations. The "Big Three" U.S. nationally recognized statistical rating organizations (NRSROs): Moody's, S&P, Fitch.

**Recourse / Non-Recourse.** A loan is recourse if the lender has a claim on the borrower's assets beyond the collateral; non-recourse if the lender's claim is limited to the collateral. U.S. residential mortgages are non-recourse in most states (the lender can claim only the home, not the borrower's other assets).

**Rehypothecation.** The practice by which a lender of collateral reuses that collateral to fund the lender's own positions. Can produce collateral chains in which the same security appears as an asset of multiple entities.

**REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust).** A corporate structure for owning real estate that, subject to qualifications, is exempt from corporate income tax in exchange for distributing most income.

**Repo (Repurchase Agreement).** A short-term collateralized loan structured as a sale of securities with an agreement to repurchase them at a later date at a slightly higher price. The dominant pre-crisis funding source for dealer balance sheets.

**Risk Retention.** A regulatory requirement that ABS issuers retain a 5% economic interest in their securitizations. Implemented under Dodd-Frank Section 941 with carve-outs for QRM and certain other categories.

**RMBS.** Residential Mortgage-Backed Security.

---

## S

**Servicer.** The institution that operates the post-origination administration of a mortgage — collecting payments, managing escrows, handling delinquencies, pursuing foreclosure.

**SFR (Single-Family Rental).** Single-family homes held as rentals, increasingly by institutional investors post-2012.

**SIFI (Systemically Important Financial Institution).** A designation under Dodd-Frank for institutions whose failure would pose risks to financial stability. Subject to enhanced supervision by the Federal Reserve.

**SIV (Structured Investment Vehicle).** A pre-crisis off-balance-sheet entity that financed long-dated structured credit holdings with a mixture of medium-term notes and ABCP, leveraged 10–15x. SIVs unwound during 2007–2008; the category essentially does not exist post-crisis.

**SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate).** The post-LIBOR reference rate for U.S. dollar transactions, based on overnight Treasury repo transactions.

**Special Purpose Entity (SPE) / Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV).** A bankruptcy-remote legal entity created to hold specific assets and issue securities backed by those assets. The legal foundation for securitization.

**Stated-Income Loan.** A loan in which the borrower's income is accepted without verification documents. Pre-crisis, approximately 50% of subprime originations were stated-income. Substantially eliminated post-crisis by the Ability-to-Repay rule.

**Subordination.** The amount of lower-rated tranches sitting beneath a particular tranche in the loss-absorption waterfall. The primary form of credit enhancement.

**Subprime.** Borrower credit quality below prime — typically FICO under 660. Subprime mortgage originations peaked at approximately $625 billion in 2005.

**Super-Senior.** The most senior tranche in a structure, typically positioned above the AAA-rated senior tranche and with even higher subordination. Pre-crisis CDO super-seniors were retained on dealer banks' balance sheets and were the principal channel of writedowns at major banks in 2007–2008.

**Synthetic CDO.** A CDO whose "collateral" is a portfolio of CDS contracts rather than cash bonds. Allows the multiplication of exposure to a fixed underlying reference set.

---

## T

**TALF (Term Asset-Backed Securities Loan Facility).** A Federal Reserve facility (initially 2008–2010; re-launched March 2020) lending against ABS and CMBS to support securitization markets.

**TARP (Troubled Asset Relief Program).** A $700 billion authority granted to Treasury by the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act of October 2008. Originally intended for troubled-asset purchases; rapidly modified in implementation to focus on bank capital injections (Capital Purchase Program), AIG support, auto industry support, and mortgage modification programs.

**TBA (To-Be-Announced).** A forward market for agency MBS in which dealers commit to deliver a specified coupon and issuer of MBS on a settlement date, with the actual pool selected at seller's option from any pool meeting "good delivery" standards. The principal liquidity venue for agency MBS.

**Tranche.** A specific class of securities issued by a securitization, with defined priority of payment and loss absorption.

**Tri-Party Repo.** A repo structure in which a clearing bank (in the U.S., almost entirely BNY Mellon and JPMorgan Chase) holds collateral and manages cash flows for both parties.

---

## V

**VaR (Value-at-Risk).** A statistical measure of the loss a portfolio would not exceed with a specified confidence level over a specified horizon. Pre-crisis dominant market risk metric; critiqued for procyclicality, thin-tail assumptions, and failure to capture stressed correlations.

**Variation Margin (VM).** Collateral posted by the losing party in a derivatives transaction to cover the realized mark-to-market loss since the last margin call.

**Volcker Rule.** A provision of Dodd-Frank (Title VI) that prohibits proprietary trading by U.S. banks and limits their investment in hedge funds and private equity funds.

---

## W

**Warehouse Line.** Short-term bank credit extended to a mortgage originator to fund loans between closing and sale to the secondary market. The principal funding source for non-bank mortgage originators.

**Waterfall.** See Cash Flow Waterfall.
